## What didn't work / gotchas (read before operating)

- **`fm-send --resolve-key` requires the key BEFORE the colon**: `needs-decision [key=x]: ...`. A worker that writes `needs-decision: [key=x] ...` creates an *unkeyed* record — resolve-key refuses and you must have the worker append its own `resolved:` line instead.
- **`fm-spawn` project positional** must be `projects/sigil` (or absolute), not bare `sigil` (fails `cd: sigil`).
- **Excel lock files** (`~$*.xlsx`) inside the repo block the guarded local merge until the captain closes the workbook window; now gitignored in sigil, but `lsof` the file before merging/tearing down anyway — teardown reaps worktree processes and Excel can hold handles there.
- **The captain edits built workbooks as change requests** (twice: Capital & S2C, DCF). Procedure that works: immediately `cp` his edited xlsx into firstmate `data/` (rebuild clobbers it), dump with openpyxl (formulas + cached values), brief the worker "his FORMAT, the model's VALUES, batch discrepancies as ONE needs-decision". His hand sheets tie out to the model remarkably well.
- **Hand-edits sit uncommitted in `projects/sigil`** — the merge guard refuses a dirty tree; get his explicit "restore" before `git checkout --` of a superseded hand-edit (never discard without his word). Same for captain-updated `raw/` data: commit his cards to main first (he approved once at `7c1345b`) so provenance doesn't dangle.
- **Stale/wedge wakes fire constantly for healthy xhigh workers** (>245s pane silence during thinking/recalc). Reconcile with `fm-crew-state.sh` (claude-hook source) + progress evidence (context %, todo list). A genuinely blocked worker surfaced as a distinct herdr "waiting on human" wake. See `data/learnings.md`.
- **Lavish background polls get killed** by the harness sometimes — just re-run; queued feedback is never lost. Later reviews were done fine in plain chat.
- **Watcher-beacon "WATCHER DOWN" banners** appeared all session while Stop-hook wakes worked perfectly; treated as cosmetic under the herdr backend — never manually arm.
